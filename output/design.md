# Storefront design and customer retention

## Brand and typography

The storefront now pairs Fraunces display headlines with DM Sans body text and Manrope product labels. Yale blue, warm paper, deep ink, and restrained brass accents distinguish Campus Customs from a generic product grid. The consistent visual identity gives returning shoppers a recognizable place to pick up where they left off, while the type pairing keeps descriptions and shopping details readable.


## Campus character

The custom bulldog drawing has been replaced with an official Yale News photograph of Handsome Dan XIX, credited there to Dan Renzetti. The full photograph is saved in `frontend/src/data/handsome-dan.jpg`, and a close crop of the same image in `frontend/src/data/handsome-dan-avatar.jpg` keeps his face visible in the small header, product card, account, and chat badges. The About page displays the wider photo. Source: [Yale News, “For Handsome Dan, a place of his own”](https://news.yale.edu/2021/10/27/handsome-dan-place-his-own). Paw-print accents remain in the hero and collection highlights. Using the same recognizable campus mascot across browsing and assistance helps returning shoppers identify the store and makes the brand feel more personal.

## Product presentation and hierarchy

Product images sit on consistent warm-toned surfaces; cards include clearer category and price hierarchy plus labeled color swatches and available-size counts. A larger editorial hero, distinctive section titles, and a campus-favorites collection make the range easier to scan. This helps shoppers compare items and return with a clearer sense of what they liked.

## Motion and feedback

Subtle entrances, a gentle hero-product float, card hover lift, and an animated chat launcher add feedback to browsing and assistance. The transitions help the storefront feel responsive and considered, which can encourage shoppers to spend time exploring. All added animation is disabled for visitors who prefer reduced motion.

## Chat personality

The assistant is presented as the Campus Guide with a bulldog avatar, warmer chat bubbles, and starter questions that change on product pages. Quick suggestions lower the effort required to ask for help; direct product links return shoppers to the items they are considering, supporting an easier path back into browsing.
